Mathetic Fellowship

Mathetic, adj. — concerned with increasing our capacity
to learn, think, create, and discover.

The Mathetic Fellowship convenes roughly five Berkeley students for a one-month social sciences (psychology, HCI, cognitive science, economics, etc.) research fellowship. Fellows will receive free Claude Max 20x, have weekly group office hours with the Mathetic team and the rest of the fellowship cohort, and have 2x/week one-on-one office hours with each of us to give feedback on building with Engelbart and to get help with your research project.

When we were first getting into research, it was super difficult to know where to get started, what to build, and how much we should be reading. What helped us most was familiarizing ourselves with and extending the projects of PhD students and professors to get a better sense of what the bottlenecks were and what knowledge we needed to learn to meaningfully contribute to research. The purpose of this fellowship is to support you in learning the nuances of your research topic through constructing prototypes, tools, and systems that help you better understand a paper's data and purpose.

While most research opportunities for undergrads require that students dedicate their time to assisting a PhD student with their existing research, the Mathetic Fellowship is specifically designed for you to work on a project of your own that you find intrinsically interesting. Through using Engelbart, our open-source harness built to support planning, understanding, and building complex research projects, you'll expand upon existing research protocols, datasets, and code to create your own research project.

Fellows should expect to spend between five and ten hours each week working on their fellowship project, which will be shared with PhD students, professors, and your peers at the end of the cohort. Fellows will also provide regular feedback on using Engelbart to plan and build their research projects.
